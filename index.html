01 / THE INTERFACE

Your motion.
The robot’s reach.

Different parts of the body move differently. HITMEN assigns each one an interface that fits—so looking, reaching, leaning, and grasping become part of one continuous action.

01

Look naturally.

HEAD → HMD

Head motion guides the robot’s gaze. Stereo vision brings the robot’s view back to you.

02

Move your torso.

CHEST → VR CONTROLLERS

Chest-mounted controllers capture how you turn and lean, independently of your head.

03

Reach directly.

ARMS → LEADER ARMS

Shoulder-mounted leader arms map your joint motion directly to the robot’s arms.

04

Keep your touch.

HANDS → VR + RGB CAMERAS

Palm-facing wrist cameras keep fingers in view when headset hand tracking struggles.

02 / ANTICIPATING OCCLUSION

Tracking fades.
Control carries on.

A hand turns. An arm crosses the chest. The headset’s view changes—and so does the quality of its tracking.

HITMEN learns from recent motion to anticipate the next second of hand and controller tracking reliability. When VR hand tracking becomes unreliable, finger control shifts toward the wrist-camera estimate.

Recent motion→Predict reliability→Adapt control

03 / INDEPENDENT HEAD & TORSO

Look one way.
Lean another.

Your gaze should not decide every movement of your body.

HITMEN gives the head and chest separate targets. The neck follows the HMD; the waist follows the chest controllers. That separation lets you change your viewpoint while keeping your torso where the task needs it.

↘

Less confidence. Less waist motion.

As controller tracking becomes less reliable, HITMEN gradually narrows the allowed waist movement. If both controller signals disappear, the waist holds its last command while the neck continues to follow the head target within its limits.

04 / HITMEN IN ACTION

Small details.
Wider possibilities.

From lining up a precise insertion to moving a hand beyond the headset’s tracking coverage, the interface makes more of the robot’s upper body available to the operator.
